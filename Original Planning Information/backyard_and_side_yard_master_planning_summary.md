## 2. Locked-In Side-Yard Fence Setback, Cost Split & Phased Gate Architecture

### Locked-In Configuration (`STUCCO_SETBACK` — `145.5 lin ft` Total: `2026 South` $\rightarrow$ `2027 North`)
* **2026 Phase 1 — South Side Fence (50% Neighbor Split) & Pedestrian Gate (100% Ours) (`wx = 57.5', wy = 41.0'–50.0'`):** Set back **$2.2\text{ ft}$ West of the SE stucco corner** (`wx = 59.7'`, or $8.2\text{ ft}$ West of the `3152` front porch stone pillar at `wx = 65.7'`) directly onto flat stucco just East of the gas and electric meters (`wx = 51.0'–54.5'`). Uses a **$4.0\text{ ft}$ pedestrian walk gate + $5.0\text{ ft}$ vinyl wing panel** (**paid 100% by us**: $\$725$ Pro / $\$425$ DIY + $\$215$ Pro / $\$160$ DIY footings/hardware), enclosing the utility meters and **completely open AC condenser (`wx = 45.0'–48.0'`, no cover or shroud)** inside the gate while allowing both the **$4.0\text{ ft+}$ wide grass strip (`wy = 43.5'–47.5'`)** and the **$2.0\text{ ft}$ South planting bed (`wy = 48.0'–50.0'`)** to continue uninterrupted under/past the gate into the front yard. Leaves **$57.5\text{ lin ft}$** of South property-line fence (`wx = 0.0'–57.5'`, **split 50/50 with our South neighbor**: our 50% share is $\$1,210$ Pro / $\$750$ DIY; **2026 Phase 1 total = $\$2,150$ Pro / $\$1,335$ DIY**).
* **2027 Phase 2 — North Side Fence (50% Neighbor Split) & Double Vehicle Gate (100% Ours) (`wx = 70.0', wy = 0.0'–9.0'`):** Set back **$3.2\text{ ft}$ West of the NE garage corner** (`wx = 73.2'`), which places the hinge/latch post **$1.2\text{ ft}$ behind the $2.0\text{ ft}$ stone masonry wrap return** (`wx = 71.2'–73.2'`) and corner downspout splash block directly onto flat stucco. Uses an **$8.0'\text{–}8.5'$ double-swing steel-frame vinyl vehicle gate** across the $9.0\text{ ft}$ corridor (**paid 100% by us**: $\$1,350$ Pro / $\$850$ DIY), cleanly separating the **white decorative rock front/side strip East of the gate (`wx = 70.0'–96.7'`)** from the **compacted road base utility/trailer pad West of the gate (`wx = 30.0'–70.0'`)**, and leaving **$70.0\text{ lin ft}$** of North property-line fence (`wx = 0.0'–70.0'`, **split 50/50 with our North neighbor**: our 50% share is $\$1,590$ Pro / $\$990$ DIY; **2027 North fence + gate total = $\$2,940$ Pro / $\$1,840$ DIY**).
* **Relative Symmetry Strategy:** Both gates step $2.2\text{ to }3.2\text{ ft}$ back from their respective front stucco corners rather than forcing street-line alignment across the stepped facade.

### Governing Spatial Equations (Reconciled Lot 3152 Side Yards)
Given side yard width $W = 9.0\text{ ft}$, North garage wall depth $D_{\text{north}} = 43.2\text{ ft}$ (`wx = 30.0'–73.2'`), South stucco wall depth $D_{\text{south}} = 29.7\text{ ft}$ (`wx = 30.0'–59.7'`), North setback $S_{\text{north}} = 3.2\text{ ft}$, and South setback $S_{\text{south}} = 2.2\text{ ft}$:

$$L_{\text{north\_enclosed}} = 43.2 - 3.2 = 40.0\text{ ft} \quad (\text{Fence run including } 30.0'\text{ backyard} = 70.0\text{ lin ft})$$

$$L_{\text{south\_enclosed}} = 29.7 - 2.2 = 27.5\text{ ft} \quad (\text{Fence run including } 30.0'\text{ backyard} = 57.5\text{ lin ft})$$

$$A_{\text{north\_enclosed}} = 9.0 \times 40.0 = 360.0\text{ sq ft} \quad (\text{Compacted road base pad West of } wx = 70.0'\text{ gate; } 28.8\text{ sq ft white rock East of gate})$$

$$A_{\text{south\_enclosed}} = 9.0 \times 27.5 = 247.5\text{ sq ft} \quad (\text{of } 267.3\text{ sq ft South stucco run})$$

$$A_{\text{total\_enclosed\_sides}} = 360.0 + 247.5 = 607.5\text{ sq ft}$$

## 3. Locked-In Open 2-Pillar Solid-Roof Patio Awning (`Option #1C`), L-Sectional & Propane Fire Table Architecture

### Locked-In Configuration (On Existing `21.0' × 12.5'` Concrete Patio — 100% Open on All Sides)
* **Location:** Placed directly on the existing **$21.0\text{ ft} \times 12.5\text{ ft}$ ($262.5\text{ sq ft}$) concrete patio slab** (`wx = 17.5'–30.0', wy = 20.0'–41.0'`), preserving **$100\%$ of the open central lawn** ($0\text{ sq ft}$ of grass lost).
* **House-Attached 2-Pillar Dark-Espresso Heavy-Timber Solid-Roof Awning (`Option #1C` — 100% Open Sides, No Privacy Wall):**
  * **East House Wall Ledger (`0` House Pillars):** $2\times 10$ or $2\times 12 \times 21.0\text{ ft}$ ledger board lag-bolted directly into the house rim joist at `wx = 30.0', wy = 20.0'–41.0'` ($\approx 9.5'\text{–}10.0'$ height) with **ZERO vertical posts against the home**.
  * **Strictly 2 Outer West Corner Pillars (`20.0'` Clear Span) & Completely Open Sides:** Two $6\times 6$ or $8\times 8$ rough-sawn Western Red Cedar / Douglas Fir posts stained **Dark Espresso / Kona Brown** anchored via matte-black Simpson Strong-Tie Outdoor Accents post bases at the outer West corners of the slab (`P1` at `wx = 18.0', wy = 20.5'` and `P2` at `wx = 18.0', wy = 40.5'`), supporting a $21.0\text{ ft}$ structural Glulam/flitch West header beam with $45^\circ$ timber knee braces, $2\times 8$ E–W rafters, a **$100\%$ opaque $1\times 6$ Tongue-and-Groove cedar wood ceiling deck underneath**, a **matte-black standing-seam architectural steel roof & front rain gutter on top**, and warm $2700\text{K}$ Edison LED bistro string lights. **All sides of the awning are completely open (zero privacy walls or slat screens on the awning)** so sightlines and airflow across the backyard and South side corridor are $100\%$ unobstructed.
* **Two-Zone Patio Furniture Layout:**
  * **North Zone (`wy = 20.0'–29.5'`, $9.5\text{ ft}$ wide):** Clear entry corridor from the $6.0\text{ ft}$ sliding glass door (`wy = 21.5'–27.5'`) and $3.0\text{ ft}$ rear compacted road base path (`wy = 9.0'–20.0'`), plus the stainless BBQ grill station (`wx = 28.0'–30.0', wy = 28.0'–31.0'`).
  * **South Zone (`wy = 29.5'–41.0'`, $11.5\text{ ft}$ wide):** **L-Shaped Outdoor Lounge Sectional** on an $8'\times 10'$ outdoor rug centered around a **Long Rectangular Dual-Purpose Propane Fire Table** ($28"\times 48"$ to $32"\times 52"$, $50,000\text{ BTU}$, concealed $20\text{ lb}$ tank, flush metal burner lid that converts the pit into a full dining/coffee table).

### Why Patio Placement Was Chosen Over a Yard Corner Pit or Paver Bump-Out

1. **Propane Eliminates Wood-Smoke Clearance Requirements:** Unlike wood-burning pits that require $15\text{ to }25\text{ ft}$ clearance from structures, a propane fire table produces zero sparks or wood smoke and operates safely on the covered patio slab under the open-sided timber awning.
2. **Avoids the "Corner Trap" in a $30.0\text{ ft}$ Deep Backyard:**
   * A dedicated yard corner fire pit requires a $12.5\text{ to }14.0\text{ ft}$ diameter gravel/paver circle ($\approx 150\text{ sq ft}$) plus a $3.0\text{ to }4.0\text{ ft}$ heat buffer from vinyl fencing and perimeter trees—pushing the pit center $9.5\text{ ft}$ out from the corner walls and colliding with the $12.5\text{ ft}$ patio in a $30.0\text{ ft}$ deep yard.
   * Keeping the fire table on the existing $262.5\text{ sq ft}$ concrete slab preserves the entire **$679\text{ sq ft}$ central emerald turf zone** (`wx = 4.5'–17.5'` West of patio and `wx = 4.5'–26.75'` North of patio), requires only **1 furniture set**, and costs **$\$0$ in extra hardscape excavation**.
